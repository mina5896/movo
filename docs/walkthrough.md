# Walkthrough script (about 7 minutes)

A suggested order for recording a demo. Start from `npm run dev` with a seeded database.

1. **The parent's side (1 min).** Open `/`, enter a first name, and ask "do u have anything sat morning for 2 kids, 5 and 7". Point out: correct programs, the 9:00 class being full, the sibling discount, no pressure. Then click 📞 and say "can someone just call me". It continues the same conversation by voice and asks for a number. Calls answer at lower effort, so they come back about a second faster.
2. **Tonight's weather (30 s).** In *Club details & rules*, post the notice "Tonight's practice is CANCELLED because of the weather". As the parent, ask "is there practice tonight its pouring": it says it's cancelled. Take the notice down afterwards.
3. **A hard case (30 s).** Text "Coach Mike yelled at my daughter at practice". It's warm, doesn't take sides, and hands off.
4. **The owner's inbox (30 s).** Open `/owner`. The coach conversation is at the top with the reason, and each family's line shows what they told us. Open it, show the text and call turns in one thread and the family card, reply as the owner, and show the reply appear in the parent chat.
5. **Handoffs shrink (1 min).** As a parent, ask "does he need cleats and shin guards for the first practice?". The club details don't say, so it hands off. In the owner's conversation, a reply is already drafted with [blanks]; *Send* stays off until they're filled in. Send "Shin guards are required, cleats recommended but sneakers are fine, bring water". *Teach the assistant this?* appears under it; click *Teach it - check it first*, and publish when it says safe. Ask again as a new parent: it answers itself.
6. **It points you at doubtful answers (1 min).** Every reply to a parent is double-checked by a second model. A doubtful one moves the conversation to *Needs you* with the concern in plain words. Open it, click *Fix it* (the correction is already written as a suggestion), then *Suggest a fix*. Show the proposed change and the new test, and click *Looks right - check it*. The before/after check starts by itself. Come back to a plain "✓ Safe to publish" and publish from the same place. (Under any reply, *Something wrong with this reply?* does the same for mistakes the reviewer missed.)
7. **Checking a change before parents see it (1.5 min).** Open *Club details & rules* and change the summer camp dates. Use *Try your changes* to ask about camp. Click *Check how this changes answers* and show the before/after page: what got better, what (if anything) got worse, side by side. Then publish. (A change to a rule or policy replays only the tests it can reach; a program's details are in every answer, so a camp change replays them all.)
8. **Trusting the scores (1 min).** Open *How it's doing*: the score per category over about 110 tests, from hand-written ones, a simulated parent, and corrections. Type "5 tests about late pickup" into *Write … tests about* and start it; a minute later they're waiting to be looked over, each with the parent's messages and what a good answer does. Keep them. Open *Spot-check the grader* and label one answer.

Close on the main point: the owner doesn't hunt for mistakes, and doesn't need to understand the assistant to fix one. They read a flag, write a sentence, and publish when it says it's safe. Every change is checked against every past mistake first.
